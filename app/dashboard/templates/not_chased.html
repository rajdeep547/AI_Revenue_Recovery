The payments we chose not to chase

A system built to recover failed payments spends real money on every message it sends, so it has to know whether that spending is worth it. The only way to know is to compare against what would have happened with no contact at all — how many of these customers would have paid on their own. That number cannot be estimated from the customers we do contact; it can only be measured by holding a slice of customers back and never messaging them: the control group (here, 30% of everyone, picked by a fixed coin-flip on the customer id). Their recovery rate is the baseline; only recoveries above that baseline — the incremental ones, the ones our contact can take credit for — count as value the system created. Without the held-back group that figure is unknowable, and every claim about the system would be a guess. The rest of this page applies the same discipline case by case: decisions where the expected value of contacting (the likely rupee gain, once the payoff is weighted by how probable a recovery is) fell below the floor we set, or where a hard rule made contact impossible or pointless. Not chasing these is the system working, not failing.
